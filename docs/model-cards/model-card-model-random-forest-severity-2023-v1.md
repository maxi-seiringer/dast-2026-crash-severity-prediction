# Model Card — STATS19 Road Accident Severity Random Forest Model 2023 V1

## Model Description

The STATS19 Road Accident Severity Random Forest Model is a supervised machine learning classifier designed to predict 
the severity of road traffic accidents using the UK Department for Transport STATS19 2023 dataset. The model was 
trained using a Random Forest classification algorithm implemented in Python and evaluated on validation and 
test datasets generated during the feature preparation workflow. The primary objective of the model is to support 
exploratory research and experimentation related to traffic safety analysis and accident severity prediction.

The model consumes casualty severity, road type, speed limit, weather conditions, light conditions, road surface 
conditions, time, day of week, number of vehicles and vehicle types derived from collision, vehicle, and casualty 
records included in the STATS19 open data release. 
The output of the model is a predicted accident severity (slight, serious, fatal) category based on the processed 
feature set.

The model artifact is stored at `outputs/models/model-random-forest-severity-2023-v1.pkl` and archived under the DOI
`https://doi.org/10.70124/m2a28-1q780` and was produced as part of the reproducible machine learning workflow documented
in the RO-Crate metadata. The training and evaluation pipeline consists of data loading, merging, feature engineering, 
model training, and evaluation scripts contained in the `src/scripts/` directory.

## Intended Use

The intended use of this model is academic research, educational demonstrations, and reproducible experimentation in 
the field of road safety analytics and machine learning engineering. The model is suitable for demonstrating 
classification workflows, feature engineering techniques, model evaluation procedures, and FAIR research data 
management practices. It may also support exploratory statistical analyses related to accident severity trends 
within the STATS19 dataset.

The model is intended for offline analytical workflows rather than operational deployment in safety-critical systems. 
Researchers may use the model to investigate relationships between environmental conditions and accident severity 
outcomes or to benchmark alternative machine learning approaches on the same dataset. The workflow additionally serves 
as an example of integrating machine learning artifacts into RO-Crate and FAIR4ML metadata ecosystems.

The model may be reused for educational exercises involving reproducibility, explainability, metadata documentation, 
and model governance. It is particularly suitable for coursework, demonstrations of Random Forest classification 
methods, and reproducible data science pipelines. Any reuse should acknowledge the limitations and licensing 
conditions described in this model card.

---

## Out-of-Scope Uses

The model must not be used for real-time traffic management, autonomous driving systems, or emergency response 
decision-making. It was not developed, validated, or certified for operational safety-critical environments where 
incorrect predictions could result in harm, legal liability, or financial loss. Predictions generated by the model 
should not be interpreted as authoritative safety assessments.

The model is also not suitable for insurance risk scoring, law enforcement profiling, or predictive policing 
applications. The STATS19 dataset reflects historical reporting practices and may contain biases or inconsistencies 
that could lead to unfair or misleading conclusions if used in high-impact decision contexts. Users should avoid 
deploying the model in systems that directly affect individuals without extensive independent validation and governance 
review.

The model should not be generalized beyond the geographic, temporal, and demographic scope of the underlying UK road 
safety data. Performance may degrade substantially when applied to data from other countries, future years, or 
substantially different road infrastructures. The model is intended exclusively for research and educational purposes 
within controlled analytical environments.

## Training Data

The model was trained using the UK Department for Transport STATS19 Road Safety Open Data 2023 release. 
The dataset includes structured records describing traffic collisions, vehicles, and casualties across Great Britain. 
Raw source files were merged and processed into train, validation, and test feature datasets using the reproducible 
preprocessing pipeline documented in the project repository.

The original dataset is distributed under the Open Government Licence v3.0 and is publicly available from the 
UK government open data portal. The data contains variables related to collision circumstances, environmental 
conditions, road types, weather conditions, and casualty severity outcomes. Feature preparation included cleaning, 
encoding, and splitting operations implemented in `src/scripts/03-prepare-features.py`.

Dataset source:

* UK Department for Transport. *Road Safety Open Data 2023*.

Primary access URL:

* https://www.gov.uk/government/statistical-data-sets/road-safety-open-data

The processed datasets used for model training are:

* `data/processed/stats19-features-train-2023-processed-v1.csv`
* `data/processed/stats19-features-val-2023-processed-v1.csv`
* `data/processed/stats19-features-test-2023-processed-v1.csv`

## Evaluation Results

Evaluation was performed using the workflow implemented in `src/scripts/05-evaluate.py`, and outputs were stored in the 
`outputs/reports/` and `outputs/figures/` directories. Metrics include precision, recall, and F1-score for 
accident severity prediction performance assessment.

The evaluation results reported below were generated from the Random Forest classifier using the STATS19 2023 processed
test dataset. The classification task predicts three accident severity categories: Fatal, Serious, and Slight. Because 
the dataset is imbalanced, weighted and macro averages are included to provide a more comprehensive assessment of model 
performance across majority and minority classes.

| Class / Metric   | Precision | Recall | F1-Score | Support |
| ---------------- | --------- | ------ | -------- | ------- |
| Fatal            | 0.0366    | 0.0761 | 0.0495   | 92      |
| Serious          | 0.3694    | 0.4094 | 0.3884   | 1617    |
| Slight           | 0.7722    | 0.7235 | 0.7470   | 4347    |
| Accuracy         | 0.6298    | 0.6298 | 0.6298   | 6056    |
| Macro Average    | 0.3927    | 0.4030 | 0.3950   | 6056    |
| Weighted Average | 0.6535    | 0.6298 | 0.6407   | 6056    |

The evaluation results indicate that the model performs substantially better on the majority “Slight” severity class 
than on the minority “Fatal” severity class. The relatively low recall and F1-score for fatal accidents reflect the 
strong class imbalance present in the STATS19 dataset and the difficulty of predicting rare high-severity outcomes. 
Future improvements may include resampling techniques, cost-sensitive learning, hyperparameter optimization, or 
alternative ensemble methods to improve minority-class prediction performance.

## Limitations

The model is limited by the quality, completeness, and representativeness of the STATS19 dataset. Traffic collision 
records may contain missing values, reporting inconsistencies, or biases introduced through manual reporting procedures.
Because the model relies entirely on historical observational data, it cannot establish causal relationships between 
variables and accident severity outcomes.

The dataset is geographically constrained to Great Britain and temporally constrained to the 2023 reporting period. 
Changes in infrastructure, driving regulations, vehicle technologies, or reporting standards may reduce model validity
over time. The model should therefore not be assumed to generalize to future traffic conditions without retraining and 
reevaluation.

Class imbalance is another significant limitation of the current implementation. Fatal accidents are relatively rare 
compared with slight severity incidents, leading to reduced predictive performance for minority classes. The model may 
therefore underperform in identifying high-severity outcomes and should not be relied upon in scenarios requiring 
accurate rare-event detection.

## Ethical Considerations

The model was developed using publicly available government data intended for transparency, research, and statistical 
analysis. Nevertheless, predictive modeling of accident severity can introduce ethical concerns related to bias, 
fairness, and misuse. Patterns present in historical datasets may unintentionally reflect social, geographic, or 
institutional inequalities.

Users should carefully assess whether model outputs could disproportionately affect specific populations or regions 
if incorporated into downstream decision systems. The model was not audited for fairness across demographic subgroups,
and no guarantees are provided regarding equitable predictive performance. Additional fairness evaluations and 
governance reviews are recommended before any broader application.

The project promotes transparency and reproducibility through documented workflows, metadata integration, and open 
licensing. Ethical use of the model requires clear communication of uncertainty, limitations, and intended scope. 
Users must avoid presenting predictions as deterministic indicators of risk or safety outcomes.
